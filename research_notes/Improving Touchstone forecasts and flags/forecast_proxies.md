# Predicting which training material helps a larger LLM, from cheap small-model runs

Reading depth, for the report writer: papers marked "read" were opened in full HTML (extraction through a summariser, first ~100k characters). Papers marked "abstract" or "snippet" were not read in full. Repo facts cite Touchstone's own [METHOD.md](/Users/nathanhe/Downloads/touchstone/.claude/worktrees/agent-a733cb471ee107784/METHOD.md) and [RESEARCH.md](/Users/nathanhe/Downloads/touchstone/.claude/worktrees/agent-a733cb471ee107784/RESEARCH.md).

## Q1. Does data valuation (Data Shapley, Beta Shapley, LAVA, OTDD, KNN-Shapley, DVRL, Datamodels, CRAIG-style selection) hold up at LLM scale?

### Takeaway
No classical valuation method has a measured dataset-versus-dataset forecast accuracy on LLMs. I found no held-out pairwise number for any of them. They were built for point-level valuation on small classifiers, mostly for finding bad points, and the methods that do run at LLM scale gain 1-2 points over random or lose to BM25 and embedding similarity once compute is counted.

### Cited Findings
- **Benchmark on small models.** OpenDataVal covers image, text and tabular data with 11 algorithms and finds no method uniformly best. Its summary table rates Data-OOB, KNNShapley, DataShapley, BetaShapley and DVRL above random on most tasks, and LOO, DataBanzhaf and Influence Function no better than random on detection tasks. It trained no LLM in the section read — [OpenDataVal](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.10577v1) (read, table summary only; per-task numbers not reached).
- **LAVA conflict.** LAVA claims significant improvement over the state of the art on detecting low-quality data, using a class-wise OT distance — [LAVA](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.00054v3) (snippet); a review of OpenDataVal says LAVA is near or below random on noisy labels and better on feature noise — [review](https://liner.com/review/opendataval-a-unified-benchmark-for-data-valuation) (secondary source). The official repo's examples are CIFAR-10 and STL-10 — [LAVA repo](https://github.com/reds-lab/LAVA) (snippet).
- **KNN-Shapley.** Exact values take O(N log N), tested on up to 10 million points, but they value data for a KNN predictor — [Jia et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08619) (abstract).
- **Datamodels.** Linear datamodels predict outputs well, at the cost of four million trained networks — [Ilyas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00622) (abstract). Proxy-scale data attribution in vision peaks at an LDS of about 0.21, which the authors call low — [Khaddaj et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16260v1) (read).
- **Shapley on fine-tuning.** TS-DShapley transfers values from a simple classifier built on the target's representations, on BERT-based models and NLU benchmarks. It reports beating other selection methods, with no numbers in the abstract — [Schoch et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10165) (abstract). A DPO-specific Shapley approximation exists; I did not reach its numbers — [Tamine et al. 2025](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2512.15765v1) (snippet).
- **OTDD.** It correlates with transfer hardness, tested on image and BERT-era experiments — [Alvarez-Melis and Fusi 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02923) (snippet). One secondary summary gives -0.85 with augmentation and -0.59 without, MNIST to USPS, an image task — [s-OTDD paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18901) (snippet). No study of OTDD predicting LLM fine-tuning gain turned up.
- **Compute-constrained selection (the strongest LLM evidence).** Over 600 models, 7B-70B, on MMLU, BBH and IFEval. "Complex data selection methods are almost never Pareto-optimal." BM25 and embedding similarity beat perplexity and LESS (gradient) at 7B and 8B. Perplexity and LESS win at 70B after about 5% of tokens. Counting only training compute, LESS leads — [Xia et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16208v1) (read).
- **LLM-scale selection with a proxy.** BLISS with a small proxy: zero-shot accuracy across 9 tasks at 1B 47.9 vs MATES 47.5 vs random 46.4; at 2.8B 49.0 vs 47.6; per-task standard errors about 0.5-1.5, results mixed by task — [BLISS](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06048v3) (read). S2L uses a reference model 40x smaller than the target; an 11% MathInstruct subset matched full-data performance — [S2L](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07384) (abstract). DsDm gains over random held until the proxy had about 4x less compute than a 760M reference, on SQuAD and LAMBADA — [Khaddaj et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16260v1) (read).

### Inferences
- All of this answers "which items to keep from one pool," not "which of two sets helps more." A dataset-level Shapley over k sets needs up to 2^k retrains and answers a coalition question Touchstone is not asking.
- Touchstone's gradient-similarity failure (development 0.59-0.72, unstable across seeds) matches the Xia result: below about 13B, gradient methods are not the efficient choice. The same family includes influence functions, so nothing here justifies reopening it.
- The one transferable result is cheap relevance: BM25 or embedding similarity between material and suite. It needs no training. Evidence is from data selection, not pairwise forecasting.
- Touchstone's existing per-rung training run is already a direct Monte Carlo sample of dataset value. Point-level approximations of that value would add noise, not information.

### Gaps
- No held-out pairwise accuracy for any listed method on any LLM. "Holds up at LLM scale, with what accuracy" has no answer in what I found.
- I did not open a CRAIG paper. S2L is the closest LLM-scale relative, and I found no CRAIG result on LLMs.
- OpenDataVal sample-size limits and per-task numbers were not reached.

## Q2. What do scaling-law and proxy studies show, and when do small proxies fail?

### Takeaway
Ranking at a single small size is the baseline to beat, and at pretraining scale it reaches about 80%. Scaling-law fits do not beat it. The successes come from pretraining from scratch with loss or likelihood metrics. Math tasks scored by accuracy stay near chance, and Touchstone's 60-step fine-tune from different pretrained bases is a harder and different setting.

### Cited Findings
- **DataDecide setup.** 25 recipes, 14 sizes (4M-1B), 3 seeds, 1,050 models, token-to-parameter ratio 100. Target winner is the mean over 3 seeds. Decision accuracy is the share of recipe pairs with the right winner — [Magnusson et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11393v2) (read).
- **DataDecide results.** About 80% correct predicting 1B from 150M. MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, MBPP and HumanEval exceed 80% with 0.01% of the compute using continuous likelihood metrics. Correct Prob lifts HumanEval and MBPP from trivial to over 80%. "No scaling law methods among 8 baselines exceed the compute-decision frontier of single-scale predictions." Results on all 25 recipes, in-sample in the sense that metrics were compared on the same testbed — [same](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11393v2).
- **DataDecide failures.** "Common math tasks remain near trivial decision accuracy regardless of metric," and exceed 80% only if the target metric also switches to Correct Prob. BoolQ gets beyond trivial only from intermediate checkpoints of the target runs. SocialIQA is hard at any scale. HellaSwag, SocialIQA and WinoGrande are insensitive until a compute threshold, then gain roughly log-linearly — [same](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11393v2).
- **Closest match to "Forecasting pretraining data decisions."** Patel et al. score next-token distributions on expert traces in one forward pass, no generation. On DataDecide, frequency-weighted top-5 accuracy gives "above 0.85" decision accuracy at about 10^-5 of target compute, against about 10^-1 for the downstream baseline. The proxy was chosen post hoc from 80 candidates, so this is effectively in-sample. On model selection across 18 models, held-out leave-2-tasks-out Spearman is 0.54 for the single proxy, against 0.36 for CE loss and 0.33 for rBridge — [Patel et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18607) (read).
- **rBridge.** Proxy NLL on frontier-model reasoning traces, weighted by the frontier model's token confidence. 80.8% decision accuracy on 25 datasets (ARC-C and CommonsenseQA, under 100M to 1.2B); at the 3.7M point, baselines sit near chance. R² from 1B to 13B averages 0.874 against 0.304 for accuracy on six tasks including GSM8K and MATH500 (train R²). Traces beat benchmark gold labels because labels carry format artefacts; filtering wrong traces helped little — [rBridge](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21013v3) (read).
- **Tiny proxy learning rate.** Standard LR proxies give Spearman below 0.75; LRs below 1e-4 give above 0.92 on three architectures; GPT2-125M to Pythia-1B exceeds 0.95 over 253 pairs of 23 recipes, 3 seeds. Standard-LR correlations are near zero or negative on many domains. Evaluated on validation loss and five downstream tasks. Theory is proven for random-feature models only; pretraining single-epoch only; over 20,000 runs — [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24503v1) (read). Listed at ICLR 2026 by [papernotes](https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/llm_pretraining/can_small_training_runs_reliably_guide_data_curation_rethinking_proxy-model_prac/).
- **Small-to-large consistency.** 10 training distributions, 6 test sets, 40M-760M. Losses correlate highly on four of six tasks; SQuAD and TriviaQA correlate less. A 57M proxy uses about 175x less compute. Proxies with near-random accuracy still correlate on HellaSwag and COPA — [Khaddaj et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16260v1) (read). A figure of mean correlation 0.89 appears only on a [third-party site](https://newx.sg/paper/detail/6fae02ab-f823-11f0-b84c-00163e10baa7), unconfirmed.
- **Data Mixing Laws.** Mixture-to-loss law with 24 fit and 8 held-out mixtures, held-out MAE 0.0074-0.0365 by domain, 70M pipeline with one 1B target. Target is loss only. Rankings change with model size and steps. Errors accumulate across nested fits — [Ye et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16952) (read).
- **Scaling Laws for Optimal Data Mixtures.** Fit on about 160 weight configurations at 412M-1.4B, held-out at 3B on 4 unseen weight vectors: mean relative error 1.30-1.39%, up to 4.45% on Wikipedia. Target is cross-entropy loss, not task accuracy — [Shukor et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09404v2) (read).
- **TuneAhead (fine-tuning).** Over 1,300 LoRA runs on Qwen2.5-7B, MMLU, 3-seed mean target, 370 held-out runs, 24 meta-features plus a 100-step probe, about 7.76 minutes per probe. RMSE 1.47, ProxyLM 2.11, early-stop extrapolation 7.43. Top features are loss stability and landscape flatness. Authors say it is not universal, and it is unclear whether test datasets were unseen — [Luo et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17660) (read). The abstract-level description and one commentary call the probe simulated; the paper body describes a real short training run — [commentary](https://groundy.com/articles/can-you-predict-a-fine-tunes-payoff-before-training-finishes/). No independent replication found.
- **Rectified Scaling Law.** Fine-tuning curves have a "pre-power phase" that pretraining laws miss; pre-learned data size is added to the law — [Lin et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02314) (abstract).
- **Learnability gap.** Models at 3B or below do not reliably gain from long chain-of-thought or strong-teacher traces; shorter reasoning helps more — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143) (snippet).
- **Touchstone's own version.** Rankings at 135M and 360M are nearly noise-free at the proxy (two seeds Spearman 0.98) yet disagree with the target, because at 360M (base 49%) the same material can lower accuracy that it raises at 135M (base 15%) — [METHOD.md](/Users/nathanhe/Downloads/touchstone/.claude/worktrees/agent-a733cb471ee107784/METHOD.md).

### Inferences
- **Failure conditions, combined:** (1) a discontinuous metric on small models; (2) a task below the proxy's competence; (3) spread between candidates smaller than run-to-run noise; (4) standard LR; (5) math scored by accuracy at the target; (6) knowledge-recall tasks (SQuAD, TriviaQA); (7) a material whose effect flips sign with base skill.
- **Touchstone sits on most of those at once**, so 70% on arithmetic is consistent with the literature, not a shortfall. DataDecide's math tasks are near chance on the same kind of target.
- **All the headline numbers are pretraining from scratch,** where every size starts from the same empty state and trains long. Touchstone starts from three different pretrained bases and trains 40-60 steps, so base skill differences are part of the signal. The closest evidence is the fine-tuning work (TuneAhead, ProxyLM, Rectified Law), which predicts magnitude with many runs and does not forecast small to large. Pretraining numbers should not be quoted as targets for Touchstone.
- **Do not build a scaling-law or extrapolation step.** DataDecide's negative result on 8 baselines removes the reason to add a rung. A third rung through 1.7B would be spent better on target-size measurement.
- **Size ratios.** 150M to 1B is about 7x; 135M to 1.7B is about 13x; 360M to 1.7B is about 5x. Khaddaj's 4x compute limit for dataset selection puts 360M to 1.7B near that limit even before the fine-tuning difference.

### Gaps
- I found no paper titled "Cost-Aware proxies." The nearest are the cost framing in [Xia et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16208v1) and the compute measure in [Patel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18607).
- Nobody reports small-to-large decision accuracy for fine-tuning from different pretrained bases, which is Touchstone's exact setting.
- Per-task DataDecide numbers exist only in figures, so I could not quote them.

## Q3. Could a different proxy help?

### Takeaway
Two options have a concrete reason to differ from what failed. One is a short fine-tune of the large model itself, scored on the held-out suite. The other is likelihood of expert reasoning traces, which gives a small model something to measure on tasks it cannot solve. Loss on the material, OTDD and influence functions are weakly supported or already covered by a failed family.

### Cited Findings
- **Truncated target run.** "Decisions from intermediate checkpoints are as good as compute equivalent final checkpoints," and BoolQ is forecastable only that way — [Magnusson et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11393v2) (read). Evidence from one short probe on the same base: TuneAhead full RMSE 1.47; early-stop extrapolation alone r 0.90 but RMSE 7.43, with only 11.2% within 1 point. Its r reflects wide spread across runs, including failures — [Luo et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17660). The same paper keeps its dataset features, with the probe, inside a learner fit on hundreds of runs.
- **Touchstone's earlier probes.** `reach_clean` (mean reward over first GRPO steps at the larger size, times pool and clean terms) fell from 0.83 to 0.57; `probe_rank` from 0.90 to 0.62, both failing permutation tests — [RESEARCH.md](/Users/nathanhe/Downloads/touchstone/.claude/worktrees/agent-a733cb471ee107784/RESEARCH.md).
- **Pretrained model with no training on the material.** ProxyLM finds that the estimated model with no fine-tuning, as a regressor feature, is close to fine-tuned proxies in many settings, with cross-dataset RMSE worse (9.95 vs 4.52 for M2M100). That is multilingual MT, predicting a model's score on a dataset, not which set teaches more — [Anugraha et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09334v3) (read).
- **Expert trace likelihood.** See rBridge and Patel in Q2. rBridge used GPT-4o traces and Patel used Llama-3.3-70B traces; Patel found proxy on traces fails on neither and did not measure the effect of weaker experts — [Patel et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18607); [rBridge](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21013v3).
- **Loss on the material under a large model.** Perplexity selection at 7B-13B lost to BM25 and embedding, became competitive at 13B after 5% of tokens, and led at 70B — [Xia et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16208v1). In TuneAhead, reference perplexity is a static feature, not among the top SHAP features named — [Luo et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17660).
- **Static embedding features.** TuneAhead's strongest negative predictors include embedding outlier ratio and approximate duplicates — [same](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17660).
- **Influence functions.** EK-FAC scales to 52B, with influence dropping near zero when key phrases are reversed — [Grosse et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03296) (snippet). Estimates are fairly accurate for shallow networks and often wrong for deep ones, varying with architecture and regularisation — [Basu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14651) (abstract). Later methods use a linear datamodelling score — [Sun et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18513v2) (snippet).
- **Constructed proxy.** IPROX builds a proxy from the target model, reporting over half cost reduction on Llama3.2 against the 3B model — [IPROX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17835) (snippet, unverified).

### Inferences
- **Short target fine-tune: first test.** It removes the reach failure, because the model doing the measuring is the one being forecast. Compute is close to a full 360M run at about one fifth of the steps, by parameter ratio. It differs from the failed probes because it scores held-out gain on the suite with no hand terms, instead of training reward multiplied by pool and clean factors. Its limits: it is no longer a small-model forecast; early steps may mostly teach format, so it must be read net of a same-length placebo; and it answers "what does the target do early," which suits Touchstone's short recipe but not long training.
- **Expert-trace likelihood: second test.** The failed `smooth_final` measures probability on the final answer, which is close to zero when the model cannot reach it. Traces give hundreds of tokens per item. The repo's Qwen2.5-1.5B judge can write traces at no extra cost, and it is also a test of a weaker expert, which no paper has measured. Risks: format-sensitive, so score the shuffled-answer placebo the same way; traces can favour material that resembles the expert's style; and literature evidence is pretraining only. Touchstone needs change in trace likelihood from before to after training, which no paper tested.
- **The target metric matters.** rBridge predicts accuracy from trace likelihood. DataDecide's Correct Prob reaches math only when the target is also soft. If Touchstone keeps accuracy as the target, expect less than the published numbers on math.
- **Cheap relevance score.** Embedding or BM25 similarity between material and suite is free on existing runs. Use one fixed formula with no fitting, since ridge fits on 13 environments already failed, and check it against the overlap screen so that leakage does not score as value.
- **Skip OTDD and influence functions.** OTDD has no LLM evidence, and class-wise labels do not map onto open answers. Influence functions are in the same family as the failed gradient work, and Touchstone's target is a set, not a point.
- **Skip learned meta-predictors.** TuneAhead needed about 1,000 training runs. Touchstone has about 60 environments.

### Gaps
- No paper compares small proxy against truncated target for fine-tuning at the same compute.
- No paper tests trace likelihood gain after fine-tuning, or trace likelihood under a 1.5B expert.
- 1.7B per-run wall time is not in the stored outputs I checked, so the cost of the truncated run must be measured.

## Q4. Which noise-reduction designs help, and how much do they raise a noise ceiling?

### Takeaway
The biggest documented gain is changing the metric, which took decision accuracy from 77.0% to 83.7% across 30 tasks. Other designs add a few points or nothing. On arithmetic, Touchstone's gap is mostly transfer and not noise, so noise work buys at most about 11 points there. On fresh tasks the target itself is noisy (ceiling 0.68-0.69), and that is where noise work matters.

### Cited Findings
- **Metric change.** Bits-per-byte in place of accuracy: 30-task SNR 10.0 to 31.5, decision accuracy 77.0% to 83.7% (150M to 1B), improved on 90% of benchmarks. GSM8K 46.0 to 76.7, Minerva MATH 51.0 to 90.0, MBPP 68.3 to 95.3, HumanEval 74.3 to 95.7 — [Heineman et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13144v1) (read). The paper does not say outright that BPB is the metric at both scales; its wording suggests it is. That would reconcile it with DataDecide's statement that math stays trivial unless the target changes too — [Magnusson et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11393v2).
- **SNR predicts decision accuracy.** R 0.791, R² 0.626 in the text, while the paper's Table 3 lists R² 0.5687, an internal mismatch — [Heineman et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13144v1).
- **Checkpoint averaging.** 68.9% to 71.3% (+2.4) and scaling-law error 1.03% to 0.86% — [same](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13144v1). Touchstone found no gain — [RESEARCH.md](/Users/nathanhe/Downloads/touchstone/.claude/worktrees/agent-a733cb471ee107784/RESEARCH.md).
- **Filtering low-SNR subtasks.** +2.6 on MMLU, +5 on AutoBencher; half of the MMLU subtasks with at least 5% label errors fall in the 20 lowest-SNR subtasks — [Heineman et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13144v1). This is the only suite-filtering result I found, and it is chosen on the benchmark being evaluated.
- **Why noise sets the limit.** A pair must differ by more than combined run-to-run noise; run SD at 1B reaches about 2 accuracy points — [Magnusson et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11393v2).
- **Control variates.** With correlation ρ, the sample needed falls by 1-ρ² — [control-variates summary of Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10563). Measured LLM saving is up to 12.2% with an off-the-shelf evaluator and 24.8% with a fine-tuned one, on human annotation, not on training-gain noise — [Zhou et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10563) (abstract).
- **CUPED.** About 50% variance reduction at Bing — [Deng et al. 2013](https://exp-platform.com/cuped/) (abstract). Etsy reports 7% on average, 27% with predicted covariates — [Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/de/codeascraft/reducing-experiment-duration-with-predicted-control-variates). Those are user-level web metrics. No study applies CUPED to training-gain comparisons.
- **Seeds.** Data sampling, initialisation and hyperparameters all move benchmarks markedly, and randomising more sources approached the ideal estimator at a 51x cost reduction — [Bouthillier et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03098) (abstract). That argues against treating a shared seed as sufficient control.
- **Touchstone baselines.** Gains are already paired per item. Noise ceiling is decision accuracy of one target seed against the mean of the others, 0.81 arithmetic, 0.69 fresh, 0.68 at 1.7B. Seeds do not lift the proxy: 0.74, 0.68, 0.61 at 1, 2, 3 seeds. Harder suites did not raise the ceiling (0.82 vs 0.84) and cut target SD from 2.8 to 2.3 — [METHOD.md](/Users/nathanhe/Downloads/touchstone/.claude/worktrees/agent-a733cb471ee107784/METHOD.md). On three placebo-tested environments, gain was 2.0 + 0.14 x (change in share of replies with an equals sign) + 19.3 if rewards are real, R² 0.88, 27 runs — [same](/Users/nathanhe/Downloads/touchstone/.claude/worktrees/agent-a733cb471ee107784/METHOD.md). Runs store per-item correctness for 300 items on `working` and `plain` prompts (seen in `out/gpu3/h317/validation_rl_h3_17.shard2.json`).

### Inferences
- **Units check.** The 0.81 ceiling is decision accuracy, not Spearman. For roughly bivariate normal scores, τ = (2/π) arcsin ρ and decision accuracy = (1+τ)/2. Spearman 0.60 then gives 0.705, matching the observed 0.70. A ceiling of 0.81 equals ρ of about 0.83, and 0.69 equals ρ of about 0.56.
- **What noise work can buy.** Arithmetic: at most 0.81 minus 0.70, and more seeds already showed no lift, so the shortfall is transfer. Fresh tasks: the ceiling alone caps any forecast at about 0.69, so it must rise before any proxy can be judged there.
- **The ceiling may be optimistic.** It resamples seeds on a fixed suite, so it ignores item sampling. With 300 items and 20% of items changing state, the SE of a gain difference is about sqrt(0.2/300) = 2.6 points, about the same as the target gain SD of 2.8. Item noise could be as large as the signal even where seeds agree. A bootstrap over items is a free check.
- **CUPED on base accuracy does nothing within a suite.** Two arms on the same suite at the same size share the same base score, so it cancels in the pairwise difference, and paired per-item gain is already a special case with coefficient 1. It helps only across suites or families, as a correction for ceiling and regression to the mean.
- **The useful control variate is a format shift.** The R² 0.88 regression suggests subtracting β × (change in format share), with β fit on placebo arms only. Unlike CUPED, this covariate is post-treatment, so real learning that changes format would be partly removed. It needs a validity check against placebo-free items. It would directly address the format-only gain problem, and it has only 27 runs on three arithmetic environments behind it. No paper tested it.
- **Common random numbers.** Variance of a difference is 2σ²(1-r). Different datasets on a shared seed probably have low r, because the batches differ. Measure r on stored runs before relying on it.
- **Item selection.** Dropping floor and ceiling items, by base accuracy at both sizes on development data, targets the same issue as subtask filtering at no GPU cost. Selection must be frozen before held-out scoring. The harder-suite failure warns against selecting by difficulty alone. Select on discrimination across seeds and arms.
- **More environments beat more seeds.** The fresh interval is ±13 points on 32 environments, so it could not detect a 5-10 point improvement. Spend budget on environment count, not seeds.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a noise ceiling before and after these interventions, so the lift in ceiling is my derivation, not a measured result.
- The DataDecide-style noise split into seed, order and item variance has no fine-tuning equivalent.
- Bouthillier's paired-comparison recommendations were not readable (PDF extraction failed), so its specific advice on paired seeds is not reported.

## Q5. What is the theoretical limit, and what gating does literature support?

### Takeaway
There is no accepted numeric band. The literature supports the idea of a floor but not a number, and the floor depends on the metric: accuracy has a hard floor, likelihood does not. Touchstone's 10-90% is a reasonable rule of its own for accuracy. For GRPO, a separate gate is needed, because zero reward means no learning signal at all, whatever the metric.

### Cited Findings
- **Metric artefact.** "Nonlinear or discontinuous metrics produce apparent emergent abilities," whereas linear or continuous metrics give smooth, predictable change — [Schaeffer et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004) (abstract).
- **Evidence for a floor.** Small models (≤1B) perform at chance on many multiple-choice benchmarks, so accuracy gives little signal, and BPB gives "dramatic improvement" on tasks they cannot solve — [Heineman et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13144v1). DataDecide reports periods of insensitivity followed by roughly log-linear gain after a compute threshold — [Magnusson et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11393v2). Fine-tuning has a "pre-power phase" — [Lin et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02314).
- **Evidence against a strict floor.** Proxies at near-random accuracy still correlate on HellaSwag and COPA by loss — [Khaddaj et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16260v1). rBridge's 3.7M model has useful decision accuracy where accuracy-style metrics sit near 50% — [rBridge](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21013v3).
- **Theory.** Wang's ordering guarantee is limited to random features, above a width threshold, below an LR bound treated as guidance — [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24503v1). I found no theory for when a task is unreachable to a small fine-tuned LLM.
- **Touchstone's floor.** Strings base 0-0.7% with reward stuck at 0; code 7-8%; word 1-2% from a truncation flaw; units base 25% and 71% was the only healthy family, still 0.50. The proposed gate is 10-90% at both sizes, checked before training, plus a token-limit check — [METHOD.md](/Users/nathanhe/Downloads/touchstone/.claude/worktrees/agent-a733cb471ee107784/METHOD.md). The repo's `scripts/h4_preflight.py` already records base accuracy, chance and truncation per family at each size.

### Inferences
- **Accuracy gate.** Binomial information peaks at p = 0.5, with p(1-p) of 0.09 at 10% or 90%, about a third of the peak. So 10-90% is defensible but arbitrary. Use chance-corrected accuracy on multiple choice. The band is a Touchstone rule, not a literature result.
- **Add a near-miss gate.** Accuracy changes only when an item crosses the line. The share of items with P(correct) in a middle band measures how many can flip, and is more direct than base accuracy.
- **GRPO has a hard limit.** With rewards all equal in a group, the group-normalised advantage is zero, so the only update comes from the KL term. If 135M's reward stays at zero, it learned nothing, and no soft metric recovers what never happened. Gate RL environments on reward rate in the first steps at the proxy (`reward_first`, already stored). SFT does not have this limit, because labels supply gradients even for a model that cannot solve the task.
- **Abstain, and report coverage.** Below the gate, say "no forecast," as with `not_checked` in reports. Report decision accuracy on covered pairs next to the share covered, or a gate can be made to look good by excluding hard pairs. The gate is set before any run.
- **Both directions of the size pair count.** A task the target saturates (at or above 90%) fails too, as the fresh design already flags.

### Ranked shortlist for Touchstone (my judgement, no result yet)

| Rank | Change | Why it differs from what failed | Evidence | GPU cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Reanalysis of stored per-item runs: item bootstrap of ceiling, same-seed correlation between arms, fixed relevance score, format-share adjustment fit on placebo arms | No training | Repo data | 0 |
| 1 | Gate plus abstain: base 10-90% at both sizes, reward above zero at proxy, truncation check | Removes the floor cases behind the 0.45 | Moderate; band is a rule of thumb | 0 |

| 4 | Lower proxy LR, by 10x | Not in the failed list; repo saw Spearman 0.43 to 0.89 on 8 and 0.58 to 0.68 on 13 sets, SFT, arithmetic-style datasets | Strong for pretraining | Reruns of proxies only |
| 5 | Report a soft target next to accuracy | Honest about what is forecastable | DataDecide, Heineman | 0 |

- **Risk on rank 4.** At a very small LR and few steps, the change in held-out loss is about η times the dot product of train and test gradients, which is the gradient-similarity family that already failed. Wang's runs are long pretraining; Touchstone's are 40-60 steps. Gains will also shrink, so pair it with a soft metric.
- **Testing discipline.** Score at most two frozen candidates on one fresh set, in at least two gated families, with more environments than h3. Multiple candidates on 17 environments is how development 0.90 became held-out 0.62. Retire a failure, as the stop rule says.
- **Realistic expectation.** On arithmetic, moving from 0.70 toward the 0.81 ceiling is the plausible best case. No evidence supports promising 0.80+ on non-arithmetic tasks from a 135M proxy. A truncated target may reach it, at the price of no longer being a small-model forecast.

### Gaps
- No literature gives a numeric base-accuracy band, and no paper reports forecast accuracy as a function of base accuracy.
- No theoretical result defines an unreachable task for fine-tuned LLMs.
- The GRPO zero-signal argument is derived from the algorithm's definition, not from a paper that tested it.
- Budget figures are rough. Only the TuneAhead probe time (7.76 minutes at 100 steps) has a published value, on different hardware and a 7B LoRA setup.
